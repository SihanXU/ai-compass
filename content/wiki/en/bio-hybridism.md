## Beliefs

Bio-Hybridism thinks the most promising neural network is the one that is actually made of neurons. Cultured brain cells grown on multi-electrode arrays spontaneously form networks, adapt their activity to stimulation and can be closed into a loop with a simulated environment. Instead of emulating biological learning on power-hungry silicon, it proposes using the real thing, wetware that rewires itself, runs on nutrients and learns continually by its nature.

It sits on the World side, since its intelligence is literally physical tissue embedded in a sensory loop, and at the Harness extreme, because a culture alone is useless without the electrodes, encoders, decoders, life support and software environment that make it a working system. It wears the most revolutionary shade on the chart: it would replace not the architecture but the material.

## Arguments

- Cultured neurons connected to a simulated game environment through electrical stimulation and recording have shown improved performance over minutes, a sign of adaptive learning in a dish.
- Biological neurons are vastly more energy-efficient than digital hardware per unit of computation, a growing concern as AI's energy demands rise.
- Wetware learns continually and locally by default, sidestepping catastrophic forgetting and the train-then-freeze cycle.

## Critiques

- Demonstrated capabilities are rudimentary, and it is unclear how to scale or program cultures toward complex tasks.
- Cultures are short-lived, variable between batches and hard to reproduce, which is the opposite of what engineering needs.
- Claims of "sentience" or "intelligence" in dishes are often overstated, and they raise ethical questions the field has barely started to address.
