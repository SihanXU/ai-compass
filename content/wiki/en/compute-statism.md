summary: "Compute is the decisive factor of production in AI, so whoever concentrates the most of it will set the frontier."

## Beliefs

Compute Statism treats FLOPs the way an old planned economy treated steel output. Algorithms come and go, datasets get scraped and re-scraped, but the size of the cluster determines what is possible this year. Progress is therefore a matter of capital allocation: build bigger data centers, secure more accelerators, and let parameters do the rest. Cleverness is welcome, but it is a multiplier on compute, never a substitute for it.

It sits near the top-left: language-leaning, because text is where compute has paid off most visibly, and firmly Monolith, because concentrated compute favors one giant run over many small systems. Its paradigm level is orthodox; this is less a revolution than the internal logic of an industry that already won the argument.

## Arguments

- Empirical scaling studies show loss improving smoothly and predictably with training compute across many orders of magnitude.
- Many celebrated algorithmic gains amount to a constant-factor compute multiplier, which the next hardware generation then eclipses.
- Capabilities such as long-context reasoning and multimodal generation have tended to appear first at the labs with the largest clusters.

## Critiques

- Treating compute as destiny concentrates research in a few institutions and turns academic work into a spectator sport.
- Efficiency advocates point to small and distilled models that match older giants, suggesting data and method matter more than raw FLOPs.
- Energy, cost and supply-chain limits give "just build a bigger cluster" a visible ceiling.
