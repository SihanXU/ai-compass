## Beliefs

Cybernetic Primitivism is a ring title for scores far toward World (x above 0.7) and far toward Harness (y below -0.7) at once. It is the bottom-right corner returned to its origins in mid-twentieth-century cybernetics: control and communication in the animal and the machine. Its holders see every intelligent behaviour, from a thermostat to a walking robot to a scientist, as a feedback loop that senses error and acts to reduce it. Representations, large models and language are optional decorations; the loop is everything.

On the chart it combines the World pole's insistence on physical interaction with the Harness pole's view that capability belongs to systems of coupled components rather than one model. Its darkest old-guard shade is fully earned: it deliberately reaches back past deep learning, past symbolic AI, to the oldest framework in the field, and asks whether we ever needed anything more.

## Arguments

- Feedback control underlies virtually every reliable physical system in engineering, from autopilots to industrial process control, with formal guarantees of stability.
- Homeostasis and error correction are a unifying principle across biology, from cells to behaviour, which suggests they are fundamental to intelligence.
- Systems built as explicit loops are analysable: their stability, robustness and failure modes can be studied mathematically.

## Critiques

- Classical feedback controllers need accurate models and setpoints designed by humans, and do not learn open-ended skills on their own.
- Saying "everything is a feedback loop" is true of so much that it offers little guidance for building systems that handle language or abstraction.
- Modern learning methods already incorporate feedback, so the primitivist position can look like a retreat rather than an alternative.
