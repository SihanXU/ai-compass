summary: "Holds that general intelligence requires a body acting in the world, and that one large embodied model should be built centrally."
---
## Beliefs

Embodied Statism takes the old embodiment thesis, that minds arise from bodies interacting with environments, and pairs it with a modern industrial program. Text and images are shadows of the world; to understand gravity, affordances and cause and effect, a system must push things and watch what happens. But unlike the scrappy bottom-up robotics of earlier decades, this camp wants the state-scale version: one big embodied foundation model, fed by fleets of robots and simulators, trained centrally and deployed everywhere.

It sits in the top-right corner of the chart, at the far World edge and the Monolith end: physical embodiment is the substance, and a unified model is the means. Its paradigm level is revolutionary. It claims the language-first route to intelligence is a detour, and that the real frontier is the action loop, where data is scarce and the hardest problems remain unsolved.

## Arguments

- Embodied multimodal models that mix robot sensor data with language and vision show positive transfer, suggesting embodiment and general knowledge reinforce each other.
- Interaction provides causal data that passive observation cannot: an agent can test hypotheses by intervening.
- Many valuable tasks, from household help to manufacturing, are physical; a disembodied model cannot do them no matter how fluent it is.

## Critiques

- Language-first camps point out that disembodied models already reason about the physical world surprisingly well, weakening the claim that a body is strictly necessary.
- Real-robot data is slow and expensive to collect, so the central program may be bottlenecked long before it reaches scale.
- Bottom-up robotics traditions argue that a single centralized brain ignores the computation done by morphology and local reflexes.
