## Beliefs

Harness Nihilism is a ring title earned when the vertical score falls below -0.85, the very bottom of the chart, regardless of horizontal position. Its holders take Harness Liberalism's slogan, "the model is the engine, the harness is the car", and push it to its limit: the engine barely matters. Swap the model and a good harness still works; swap the harness and the same model can go from useless to state of the art. Tool interfaces, memory, context construction, retries, verification and workflow are where performance is actually made.

The nihilism is aimed at the model, not at progress: model releases are noise, and the leaderboard of base models is a sideshow. Sitting at the bottom edge, it is the polar opposite of Compute Theocracy at the top. Its moderately revolutionary shade reflects that it inverts the field's usual hierarchy, in which models are the research and systems are engineering.

## Arguments

- Designing a better agent-computer interface, with purpose-built commands and feedback, substantially raised the same model's success rate on real software engineering tasks.
- Agents that store learned skills as code in a growing library and use automatic curricula have far outperformed the same model prompted without that scaffolding.
- Harness improvements can be shipped in days, tested cheaply and kept when models change, whereas model gains require months of training.

## Critiques

- Each new model generation has absorbed tricks that harnesses once supplied, from chain-of-thought prompting to tool selection.
- A harness cannot make a model do what it fundamentally cannot; the ceiling is still set by the weights.
- Heavily engineered harnesses become brittle, model-specific and hard to maintain, which undercuts the claim that models are interchangeable.
