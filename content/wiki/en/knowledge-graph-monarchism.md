## Beliefs

Knowledge-Graph Monarchism believes truth comes in triples: subject, relation, object. Paris, capital of, France. A well-curated graph of entities and typed relations is precise, editable, queryable and auditable, everything a neural network's weights are not. When a fact changes, you edit one edge instead of retraining a model and hoping. Language models may be eloquent courtiers, but the crown belongs to the graph, and every answer should be traceable to a node someone can inspect.

It sits near the Language edge, since graphs are built from named entities and linguistic relations, and in the middle band: a central structured store rather than a monolithic model or a sprawling agent system. Its paradigm level is old guard. Knowledge graphs powered semantic web initiatives, enterprise search and question answering long before large language models, and the monarchists see no reason to abdicate.

## Arguments

- Structured graphs support exact queries, multi-hop joins and constraint checking that free-text retrieval and neural recall handle unreliably.
- Facts can be updated, versioned and attributed individually, which matters for compliance and correctness.
- Knowledge graph embeddings and graph-grounded generation can reduce hallucination by anchoring answers to verified entities.

## Critiques

- Building and maintaining large graphs is labor-intensive, and coverage always lags the messy long tail of real knowledge.
- Much knowledge, such as procedures, nuance and context, does not fit cleanly into triples.
- Retrieval and long-context camps argue that indexing raw documents gets most of the benefit without the schema-design burden.
