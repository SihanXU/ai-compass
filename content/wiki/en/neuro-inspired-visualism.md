## Beliefs

Neuro-Inspired Visualism reminds the field where convolutional networks came from. Hierarchies of simple and complex cells, local receptive fields, increasing invariance from layer to layer: these ideas came from recordings of the visual cortex, and they worked. The brain still sees far more robustly than any model, recovering from occlusion, adversarial noise and novel viewpoints with a fraction of the data and energy. So keep studying it: recurrence and feedback, sparse coding, lateral connections, spiking dynamics, and train models that are measurably brain-like.

It sits right of center on the World side, a little above the middle of the vertical axis, favoring structured architectures over either pure scale or tool-based systems. Its paradigm level is Beyond Revolutionary. It does not accept that the current recipe of feedforward transformers on huge datasets is the end state and expects biology to supply the next big architectural idea.

## Arguments

- Hierarchical convolutional networks optimized for object recognition predict neural responses in primate visual cortex, showing a real correspondence between model and brain.
- Biological vision is far more robust to distribution shift and adversarial perturbation than standard networks, which suggests missing principles.
- Brain-inspired mechanisms such as feedback and recurrence improve performance on hard cases like occlusion and clutter.

## Critiques

- Scale-first camps note that the biggest recent gains came from data and compute, not from new neuroscience.
- Neuroscience knowledge of cortical computation remains incomplete, so "brain-inspired" can be a loose metaphor.
- Brain-likeness scores and task performance do not always move together, making the goal ambiguous.
