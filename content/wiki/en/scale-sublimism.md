## Beliefs

Scale Sublimism is a ring title awarded when two conditions hold together: a strong Monolith score (y above 0.7) and a strongly conservative paradigm score (p below -0.6). It belongs to those who find the scaling curve not just useful but sublime, an awe-inspiring line that keeps going up. Its creed is "just a little bigger": no new architecture is needed, no new objective, no exotic harness, only the same pretraining-plus-post-training recipe with one more order of magnitude of compute, data and parameters.

It sits on the top edge of the chart, committed to the single end-to-end model and comfortable on either side of the Language-World axis. What distinguishes it from Compute Theocracy is the paradigm dimension: where the theocrat worships compute itself, the sublimist is specifically loyal to the current recipe and sceptical of anyone promising a revolution.

## Arguments

- Compute-optimal scaling studies showed that balancing model size and training tokens yields large gains from the existing recipe without architectural changes.
- Many proposed alternatives to the Transformer have failed to beat a well-tuned, scaled-up Transformer when compared at equal compute.
- Incremental scaling has delivered steady, predictable improvement, while paradigm bets have high variance and long timelines.

## Critiques

- "A little bigger" each time adds up to enormous cost, and returns per dollar may be falling even while absolute capability rises.
- Paradigm conservatism risks missing the next shift, just as earlier techniques were scaled past their usefulness before being replaced.
- Some weaknesses, such as continual learning, reliable reasoning and grounding, show little sign of being solved by size alone.
