## Beliefs

Symbolic Restorationism waits in exile for the return of the rightful dynasty. Before the deep learning era, AI meant symbols: explicit representations of objects and relations, rules that manipulate them, and search over the space of possibilities. That program produced theorem provers, expert systems, planners and the physical symbol system hypothesis, the claim that symbol manipulation is both necessary and sufficient for general intelligence. Neural networks, in the restorationist view, are impressive pattern matchers that still cannot guarantee correctness, generalize systematically or explain themselves.

It sits close to the Language edge, since symbols are a formal language, and at the very bottom, the Harness end: intelligence assembled from explicit, inspectable rules, search and parts rather than one learned model. Its paradigm level is Ultra-Revolutionary. Reviving symbolic AI is a direct challenge to today's neural mainstream, and it campaigns for that return whenever a neural model hallucinates a fact or fails at a simple logic puzzle.

## Arguments

- Symbolic systems offer guarantees: a sound logical inference is correct by construction, and a verified program does what its specification says.
- Explicit representations generalize systematically to novel combinations, a known weak point for statistical models.
- Every step of symbolic reasoning can be traced and audited, which matters in law, medicine and safety-critical systems.

## Critiques

- Hand-built knowledge bases proved brittle and expensive, and struggled with the ambiguity and noise of the real world.
- The symbol grounding problem remains: symbols mean nothing unless connected to perception, which is exactly what neural networks do well.
- Neuro-symbolic camps argue that pure restoration is unnecessary; hybrids keep the benefits without abandoning learning.
