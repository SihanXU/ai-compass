## Beliefs

VLA Monarchism believes the fastest route to general robots runs through the internet. Take a vision-language model that already knows what a mug, a drawer and "put it away" mean, then train it to emit robot actions as just another output. The result is a crowned head: one model that reads the camera, understands the instruction and drives the motors. Planners, perception modules and skill libraries become courtiers at best.

It sits near the top of the chart, left of centre. The output is physical action, yet the doctrine's legitimacy comes from language, which pulls it toward the Language side: the backbone is a language-trained model and instructions arrive in words. The Monolith stance is strong, since the whole point is a single end-to-end policy. Its paradigm level is Orthodox: it extends the pretrained-backbone-plus-scale recipe to robots, even if its scaling behavior is still being mapped.

## Arguments

- Vision-language-action models transfer web-scale semantic knowledge to robots, generalizing to objects and instructions never seen in robot data.
- A single policy across tasks lets robot data from many settings reinforce shared skills.
- Open VLA models and flow-matching action heads have made the approach reproducible and capable of fairly dexterous control.

## Critiques

- Language backbones bring semantic knowledge but little sense of force, contact or timing, which dominate real manipulation.
- Large VLA models are slow to run, making high-frequency control awkward without extra machinery.
- Robot data remains scarce, so rivals doubt that one monarch can rule hardware it has barely seen.
